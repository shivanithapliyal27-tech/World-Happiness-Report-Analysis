# World Happiness Report Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes the World Happiness Report dataset using Excel-based data analysis and visualization.

The objective is to explore happiness levels across countries and years and understand how happiness relates to economic and social factors.

The analysis focuses on simple, clear visualizations that communicate important patterns in the data.

---

## Dataset

The dataset used in this project is based on the World Happiness Report.

The dataset contains information for multiple countries across several years.

Important variables include:

- Year
- Rank
- Country name
- Life evaluation (3-year average)
- Log GDP per capita
- Social support
- Healthy life expectancy
- Freedom to make life choices
- Generosity
- Perceptions of corruption

---

## Main Happiness Measure

The primary measure used in this analysis is:

**Life evaluation (3-year average)**

This measure is used as the main indicator of happiness and well-being.

---

# Analysis and Visualizations

## 1. Top 10 Happiest Countries – 2025

A horizontal bar chart is used to display the top 10 countries according to their 2025 happiness ranking.

### Purpose

To identify the countries with the highest happiness rankings in 2025.

### Key Takeaway

Finland is ranked as the happiest country in the 2025 data, followed by other highly ranked countries.

---

## 2. Average Happiness Score by Year

A line chart is used to show the average happiness score across the available years.

### Purpose

To understand how the average happiness level has changed over time.

### Key Takeaway

The yearly average provides an overall view of changes in reported life evaluation across the countries included in the dataset.

---

## 3. Average Happiness by GDP Quartile – 2025

The 2025 countries are divided into four groups based on their GDP per capita values.

The groups are:

- Q1 – Lowest GDP group
- Q2 – Lower-middle GDP group
- Q3 – Upper-middle GDP group
- Q4 – Highest GDP group

The average happiness score is then compared across these groups.

### Purpose

To examine the relationship between economic conditions and happiness.

### Key Takeaway

Countries in higher GDP groups generally show higher average happiness scores, indicating a positive relationship between economic conditions and life evaluation.

---

## 4. Average Happiness by Social Support Quartile – 2025

The 2025 countries are divided into four groups based on social support.

The average happiness score is compared across these groups.

### Purpose

To examine whether stronger social support is associated with higher happiness.

### Key Takeaway

Countries with stronger social support generally have higher average happiness scores.

---

# Methodology

The following steps were used in the analysis:

1. Imported the World Happiness Report dataset into Excel.
2. Reviewed the available variables and years.
3. Used `Life evaluation (3-year average)` as the main happiness measure.
4. Filtered the dataset to 2025 where required.
5. Used country rankings to identify the top 10 happiest countries.
6. Calculated yearly average happiness scores.
7. Divided 2025 countries into GDP quartiles.
8. Divided 2025 countries into social support quartiles.
9. Created charts to communicate the results.
10. Added short takeaways and an overall narrative.

---

# Why These Chart Types Were Used

### Bar Chart

A bar chart is suitable for comparing countries because it makes differences between individual countries easy to see.

### Line Chart

A line chart is appropriate for showing changes and trends over time.

### Column Chart

Column charts make it easy to compare average happiness between different GDP and social-support groups.

### Why a Pie Chart Was Not Used

A pie chart is generally used when categories represent parts of one complete whole.

GDP, social support, and other happiness-related factors are not parts that add up to 100% of happiness. Therefore, comparison charts are more appropriate for this analysis.

---

# Overall Narrative

The analysis shows that happiness varies across countries and over time.

The 2025 ranking highlights the countries with the highest levels of life evaluation. The yearly analysis provides an overview of how average happiness has changed across the available years.

The GDP analysis suggests that countries with stronger economic conditions tend to have higher average happiness scores. Similarly, stronger social support is associated with higher average life evaluation.

Overall, the findings suggest that national well-being is influenced by multiple economic and social factors rather than a single factor.

---

# Key Findings

- Finland ranks highest in the 2025 happiness ranking.
- Happiness levels vary across countries.
- Average happiness changes across the years.
- Higher GDP groups generally show higher average happiness.
- Stronger social support is generally associated with higher happiness.
- Economic and social conditions both play an important role in well-being.
